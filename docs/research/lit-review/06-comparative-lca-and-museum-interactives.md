# 06 — Comparative LCA and Museum Sustainability Interactives

## Scope

This review covers two interlinked bodies of literature that the IAAC tabletop installation depends on:

1. **Comparative LCA studies** for housing built with traditional masonry, 3D-printed concrete or earth, and modular prefab — the actual numbers that need to appear on the projector.
2. **Museum and science-center precedents** for interactive sustainability and construction exhibits — what works pedagogically and what gets evaluated as effective.

The review is deliberately conservative: every per-m² number is tagged with its source tier (peer-reviewed, EPD/database, or grey/marketing) so the team knows which figures are defensible to a jury and which are illustrative only.

## Search strategy

WebSearch + WebFetch on Journal of Cleaner Production, Building & Environment, Energy & Buildings, ScienceDirect, MDPI, Springer, Nature Scientific Reports for LCA; ITeC and BEDEC for the Catalan baseline; institutional sites for Climate Museum, Triennale, Vitra, Cooper Hewitt, mudac, Disseny Hub, Eden Project, California Academy of Sciences, Ars Electronica; Routledge / Wiley / SEGD for visitor-studies literature (Falk & Dierking, Allen). Searches were run May 2026. Marketing claims by 3DCP vendors (ICON, Apis Cor, COBOD) are flagged where used and never substituted for peer-reviewed numbers.

## Part A: Comparative LCA for housing construction methods

### Key works (annotated)

### Mohammad et al. (2023) — *A systematic review of life cycle assessments of 3D concrete printing*, Cleaner Materials (ScienceDirect)
Synthesises ~30 LCA studies of 3DCP. Headline conclusion: claims of carbon savings are highly sensitive to (a) the cement content of the printable mix, (b) whether reinforcement is included in the boundary, and (c) the comparative baseline. Many studies omit rebar or steel mesh, which artificially favours 3DCP. URL: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666412723000132

### Alhumayani et al. (2020) — *Environmental assessment of large-scale 3D printing in construction: A comparative study between cob and concrete*, Journal of Cleaner Production
Frequently cited comparison of 3D-printed cob (earth) vs printed concrete vs conventional masonry walls. Cob walls reduced GWP by ~80% vs printed concrete. Important caveat: thermal performance and lifespan of cob assumed equivalent, which is contestable.

### Kuzmenko et al. (2020) / Bhattacherjee et al. (2024) — *3D Concrete Printing Sustainability: A Comparative Life Cycle Assessment of Four Construction Method Scenarios*, Buildings 10(12), 245 (MDPI)
One of the cleanest four-way comparisons (cast in situ, precast, 3DCP wall, 3DCP optimised wall). Found 3DCP advantage only when geometry optimisation reduces material volume — straight printed walls underperform conventional. URL: https://www.mdpi.com/2075-5309/10/12/245

### Findings (2024) — *Comparison of Embodied Carbon of 3D-printed vs. Conventionally Built Houses*
Reports averages of **58 kg CO₂-eq/m² for 3D-printed houses vs 147 kg CO₂-eq/m² for conventional houses**. The factor-of-2 gap is unusually large and the authors attribute it to material quantity reduction; should be treated as upper-bound benefit. URL: https://findingspress.org/article/89707

### Recent ScienceDirect study (2026) — *On the sustainability of digital construction: Whole building life cycle carbon emissions according to three construction techniques*
Models 3DPC vs stick-frame (STF) vs concrete masonry unit (CMU) across four U.S. climate zones, 75- and 100-year service lives. Over 100 years, embodied carbon of 3DPC homes is **10% lower than CMU and 5% lower than STF**, with operational savings of 2–13% depending on climate. The most defensible recent number — modest, not revolutionary. URL: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2352710226004845

### Younis et al. / Liu et al. (2021) — *Comparative life cycle assessment of cross laminated timber building and concrete building with special focus on biogenic carbon*, Energy & Buildings
Found CLT building had lower impact in 11 of 18 LCA categories. Embodied GHG of CLT reduced by **46.5% in production+construction stage** vs concrete equivalent; primary energy reduced 32.5%. Biogenic carbon accounting drives much of the gap — strip it out and savings shrink to ~20–30%. URL: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0378778821008884

### Hemmati et al. (2024) — *Comparison of Embodied Carbon Footprint of a Mass Timber Building*, USDA Forest Products Lab / Buildings
Mass timber building: **198 kg CO₂-eq/m²** vs steel **243 kg CO₂-eq/m²**. Mass timber alternative achieves ~48 kg CO₂-eq/m² lower mean embodied carbon vs concrete counterpart. URL: https://www.fpl.fs.usda.gov/documnts/pdf2024/fpl_2024_hemmati002.pdf

### Wang et al. (2024) — *Comparative analysis of embodied carbon in modular and conventional construction methods in Hong Kong*, Scientific Reports 14
High-rise concrete modular (MiC) shows ~6% GWP reduction vs site-built initially, rising to **20–26% lower annual emissions** when modules are reused over a 25–50 year second life. Reuse — not prefabrication itself — is the carbon lever. URL: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-73906-7

### Pomponi & Moncaster (2016, 2017) — *Scrutinising embodied carbon in buildings: The next performance gap made manifest*, Journal of Cleaner Production
The methodological backbone. Documents that LCA results for nominally identical buildings vary by factors of 2–4 due to system boundary choices, biogenic carbon treatment, transport assumptions, and end-of-life scenarios. Required reading before any number is put on a public projector. URL: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S136403211730998X

### De Wolf, Hertwich et al. (2023) — *Reducing embodied carbon in structural systems: A review of early-stage design strategies*, MIT preprint
Current consensus on mitigation hierarchy. Reinforces Pomponi's caution: comparative claims must be on units of structural performance, not mass. URL: https://dspace.mit.edu/bitstream/handle/1721.1/152266/2023-06-09%20preprint.pdf

### TECLA (Mario Cucinella Architects + WASP, 2021) — *3D-printed earth house, Massa Lombarda*
Built precedent, **not** a peer-reviewed LCA. 60 m² shell, 200 hours print time, ~6 kW average draw (~1200 kWh total printing energy), zero-waste local-soil mix. Useful as a vendor case study and image source; do **not** quote a per-m² CO₂ figure from project PR. URL: https://www.3dwasp.com/en/3d-printed-house-tecla/

### DFAB House (NCCR Digital Fabrication / ETH Zurich, 2019)
Digitally planned and robotically built demonstrator on the NEST building (Empa). Demonstrates Mesh Mould, Smart Slab, Smart Dynamic Casting, Spatial Timber Assemblies. Long-term monitoring is published in NCCR reports; stand-alone full-LCA papers per element exist (e.g., Smart Slab embodied-carbon comparisons claim ~70% material reduction in slab) but should be cited per element, not per house. [unverified for whole-house LCA] URL: https://dfab.ch/ktt-projects/dfab-house

### Banco BEDEC / ITeC (Catalonia, updated 2025–2026)
The defensible Catalan/Spanish baseline. Contains environmental indicators (CO₂, manufacturing energy, waste, recycled content) for ~800,000 construction items aligned with EN 15804:2012+A2:2020. The 2026 release expanded to 22 environmental indicators. **This is what the team should quote for masonry numbers in a Barcelona context** rather than UK/US averages. URL: https://en.itec.cat/services/bedec/

### Headline numbers the team can use (with sources)

The team should present these as ranges, not point values, and always cite. Numbers below are cradle-to-gate (A1–A3) per m² of gross floor area unless noted.

For the Barcelona context, replace generic numbers with BEDEC line items: BEDEC contains EPD-aligned values for fired clay block, hollow concrete block, and CLT panels supplied to Spanish sites and is the only source a Catalan jury will treat as locally defensible.

### Methodological caveats the team must disclose

Pomponi (2017), De Wolf (2023), and Hertwich (2019, *Environmental Research Letters*) converge on five non-negotiable disclosures the projector caption should include:

1. **System boundary.** Is reinforcement counted? Foundations? Finishes? Many 3DCP advantage claims silently exclude rebar and conventional roof/floor systems.
2. **Lifespan.** A 100-year masonry wall vs a 50-year printed wall is not a fair per-m²-per-year comparison. Service life assumption changes results by factors of 2.
3. **Biogenic carbon.** CLT advantage shrinks substantially if biogenic uptake is excluded or end-of-life combustion is counted.
4. **Functional unit.** Compare on equal structural and thermal performance, not equal mass or equal m².
5. **Geography and grid mix.** Catalan electricity (~150 g CO₂/kWh) vs Polish (~600) changes printed-concrete energy emissions by 4×.

For a children/decision-maker audience the team should pre-package these five caveats as a single secondary projection layer titled "why these numbers wobble" — this is itself an exhibit value-add (per Allen 2004).

## Part B: Museum and exhibition precedents

### Key precedents (annotated)

### The Climate Museum, NYC (2018–present, permanent home 2031)
The first US museum dedicated to climate. Exhibitions to study: *The End of Fossil Fuel*, *Taking Action*, *In Human Time*. *Taking Action* is closest to the IAAC brief — staffed largely by high-schoolers, hands-on solutions framing, explicit invitation to group action. The lenticular print (emissions vs vulnerability) is a clean precedent for two-state physical data visualisation. URL: https://www.climatemuseum.org/work/exhibitions

### Triennale di Milano, *Broken Nature* (2019, curator Paola Antonelli)
XXII Triennale, March–September 2019. ~120 design/architecture/infrastructure projects on restorative design. Catalogue (Electa) is a strong reference for how to frame construction as one strand of broader human–environment repair rather than as an engineering optimisation problem. URL: https://triennale.org/en/events/broken-nature

### Cooper Hewitt, *Nature — Design Triennial* (2019–2020, with Cube Design Museum NL)
60+ works on synthetic biology, urban agriculture, alternative materials. Co-organisation with Cube is precedent for a touring / dual-venue model. URL: https://www.cooperhewitt.org/channel/nature/

### Cooper Hewitt, *Designing Peace* (2022–2023)
Less directly relevant to construction but methodologically interesting: object + situated story + call-to-action triad that repeats per case study. Good template for the IAAC three-method comparison structure. URL: https://www.cooperhewitt.org/2022/04/07/designing-peace-to-open-in-june-at-cooper-hewitt/

### Vitra Design Museum, *Plastic: Remaking Our World* (2022) and *Garden Futures: Designing with Nature* (2023)
*Plastic* explicitly pairs problem objects with bio-alternatives (algae bioplastics, mycelium, Klarenbeek & Dros 3D-printed composites). Closest precedent for "show the conventional, show the alternative, show the trade-offs." *Garden Futures* (with Formafantasma) reframes a familiar object (the garden) as sustainability infrastructure. URL: https://www.design-museum.de/

### mudac, Lausanne (Plateforme 10)
Cross-disciplinary contemporary design museum. Hosts roundtables on design + sustainability + digital culture. Good precedent for pairing exhibits with scheduled discursive programming. URL: https://mudac.ch/en/

### Disseny Hub Barcelona, *Matter Matters: Designing with the World* (opened Feb 2025)
Most directly relevant local precedent. Materials-and-sustainability exhibition in Barcelona. IAAC reportedly involved (per IAAC press). The team should visit and document curatorial choices, label hierarchy, and dwell-time observations before final design freeze. URLs: https://www.dissenyhub.barcelona/ and https://iaac.net/matter-matters-iaac-disseny-hub-barcelona/

### Ars Electronica Festival (Linz, annual)
Multi-year sustainability programming. *Welcome to Planet B*, *HOPE*, *Panic*, *Connected Earth*, *A New Digital Deal* — each a year-themed festival with installations, lectures, workshops. Mobile Globe with Earth-from-Space app and Tega Brain / Sam Lavigne's data installations are useful references for projector + tracked-object interaction patterns. URL: https://ars.electronica.art/

### Eden Project, Cornwall (2001–present)
~1 million visitors/year, biome-based, free-choice learning. Internal evaluation (2010–2011 sustainability report; Annual Review 2022–23) reports visitors self-reporting lifestyle reevaluation on recycling/upcycling/repair. Important caveat: self-report is a weak evidence tier — distinguish from controlled studies. URL: https://www.edenproject.com/

### California Academy of Sciences, *California: State of Nature* (2025, AAM Museum Impact Award)
Children-focused, hands-on, multi-sensory, indigenous partnerships, mock wildlife crossing tunnel. *Your Plan, Your Planet* (with Google) is a sustainability decision tool with documented use. URL: https://www.calacademy.org/exhibits/california-state-of-nature

### Guggenheim, *Countryside, The Future* (Koolhaas / AMO, 2020)
Not a sustainability exhibit per se but the fifth-floor climate maps (*Half Earth* / *Shared Planet*) are widely studied as a precedent for data-as-spatial-experience at room scale. URL: https://www.guggenheim.org/exhibition/countryside

### Falk & Dierking — *The Museum Experience Revisited* (Routledge, 2013/2016)
The standard reference for the Contextual Model of Learning (personal × social × physical contexts) and for concepts of museum fatigue, object satiation, and holding power. Directly applicable: a tabletop comparison installation must respect ~30s median engagement, design for groups not individuals, and assume prior-knowledge variance. URL: https://www.routledge.com/The-Museum-Experience-Revisited/Falk-Dierking/p/book/9781611320459

### Allen, S. (2004) — *Designs for Learning: Studying Science Museum Exhibits That Do More Than Entertain*, Science Education 88(S1)
Four design criteria the IAAC team should self-test against: **immediate apprehendability, physical interactivity, conceptual coherence, diversity of learners**. Allen also documents that exhibits with multiple interactive features have characteristic pitfalls — feature creep reduces learning. URL: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/sce.20016

### What makes a sustainability exhibit work (evidence-based)

Synthesising Falk & Dierking, Allen, the Eden Project evaluation literature, and the AAM-recognised California Academy work:

- **Apprehendable in <10 seconds.** Visitors decide whether to engage in the first few seconds. The IAAC three-zone tabletop should make the comparative question legible without reading any label.
- **Physical interactivity beats screen interactivity** for retention, but only when the physical action is conceptually tied to the learning goal. Picking up an ArUco-tagged block to swap a method satisfies this; sliders that "do something" but mean nothing don't.
- **Conceptual coherence over feature richness.** Allen's pitfall: too many interactive features dilute learning. Three methods, three phases, two trade-off axes is already at the limit.
- **Designed for social learning.** Falk & Dierking: most museum learning is conversational, not solo. The tabletop must accommodate 3–5 simultaneous viewers and prompt them to talk to each other, not just to the projector.
- **Calls to action close the loop.** Climate Museum's *Taking Action* and Eden's lifestyle-reevaluation outcomes both feature explicit invitations to do something next. A QR exit step ("download the LCA spreadsheet for your own brief") is consistent with this evidence.
- **Disclose uncertainty.** A sustainability exhibit that hides methodological wobble loses credibility with the architect/decision-maker audience. Pomponi's caveats become an exhibit feature, not a caption.

## Cross-cutting themes

- **Comparative LCA is unstable.** The three methods don't have stable rank order across studies; rank depends on geometry, lifespan, biogenic accounting, grid mix, and reuse assumptions.
- **Reuse and lifespan dominate.** Modular's biggest carbon win comes from second-life reuse (Wang 2024); CLT's biggest win comes from biogenic accounting and lifespan; 3DCP's biggest win comes from geometry optimisation that reduces material volume.
- **Local data beats global averages** for credibility with a Catalan jury — BEDEC over Inventory of Carbon and Energy (ICE) over US RMI averages.
- **Evidence-based exhibit design is a small literature** dominated by a handful of authors (Falk, Dierking, Allen, Serrell). The team should treat their criteria as design constraints, not nice-to-haves.
- **Most cited "successful" sustainability exhibits lack rigorous outcome evaluation** — existence is verifiable, effectiveness usually isn't. Eden's self-report data and the California Academy's AAM award are the strongest outcome evidence found.

## What's missing in this literature

- **No published peer-reviewed LCA of a built TECLA-class earth-printed house.** Vendor numbers only.
- **No directly comparable three-way (masonry / 3DCP / modular) LCA on the same plan, same climate, same lifespan.** Most studies are pairwise. The team's exhibit synthesis is therefore itself a small contribution.
- **No public evaluation literature** for the Climate Museum, mudac, or Disseny Hub specifically on dwell time, retention, or behaviour change.
- **No standard for tabletop projector + AR exhibit dwell time** — Allen and Falk literature is mostly on full-room or kiosk exhibits.
- **Cost data is patchy and rarely peer-reviewed.** Marketing claims of "50% cheaper 3D-printed houses" do not survive scrutiny.

## Direct recommendations for the team

1. **Use BEDEC for the masonry baseline** and cite line items by code on the projector, not just "brick."
2. **Default to the conservative LCA range** (e.g., 3DCP modest 5–10% advantage, CLT 30–40% advantage with biogenic, modular 5–10% initial / 20–25% with reuse). Show the optimistic numbers as a separate "best case" layer.
3. **Embed Pomponi's five caveats as a permanent secondary projection layer**, toggled by a physical pebble or token.
4. **Visit *Matter Matters* at Disseny Hub** before final design freeze; document label hierarchy and dwell time.
5. **Self-test the prototype against Allen's four criteria** (apprehendability, interactivity, coherence, diversity) and report scores in the project documentation.
6. **Plan for social-group use** (3–5 simultaneous viewers, conversational prompts) per Falk & Dierking.
7. **Add an explicit call to action** (download spreadsheet, design-brief checklist) per Climate Museum precedent.
8. **Distinguish "exhibit existed" from "exhibit worked"** in the team's own precedent slides — credibility with the jury.
9. **Treat TECLA, DFAB House, ICON, and Apis Cor as image precedents only**; do not put their press-release CO₂ numbers on the projector.
10. **Document your own LCA boundaries on the exhibit itself.** Visitors can see the method; the meta-message of disclosure is the strongest sustainability lesson available.
