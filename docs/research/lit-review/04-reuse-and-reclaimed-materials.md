# 04 — Reuse, Reclaimed Materials, and Circular Construction

## Scope

This review surveys academic and practitioner literature on reuse-based construction, reclaimed-material assembly, and circular construction, with particular attention to comparative LCA evidence against new-build (concrete, 3D-printed concrete, glulam). It is written to inform a 6-person IAAC MRAC + MAAI team deciding whether to add a "reclaimed brick / reused element" method as a 4th comparator alongside masonry, 3D-printed concrete/earth, and modular prefab in their tabletop installation.

## Search strategy

Searches were run via WebSearch across EPFL Infoscience, ScienceDirect, MDPI, ResearchGate, ArchDaily, the European Circular Economy Stakeholder Platform, gramaziokohler.arch.ethz.ch, and uni.li. Search terms combined lab/author names (Fivet, Brütting, De Wolf, Pomponi, Stockhammer, Weinand, Settimi, Gramazio Kohler) with concept terms ("reuse," "reclaimed," "embodied carbon," "LCA," "material passport," "construction and demolition waste"). Practitioner platforms (Madaster, BAMB, Concular/Restado, baubüro in situ) were searched directly. EPFL CRCL's "Discrete Wood" line could not be cleanly separated from EPFL IBOIS's Augmented Carpentry — IBOIS sources are used and flagged.

## Key works (annotated, in chronological order)

### Brütting, Desruelle, Senatore & Fivet, 2019 — Design of Truss Structures Through Reuse
- **Tier:** Peer-reviewed (Structures, Vol. 18, 128–137).
- **Citation:** Brütting, J., Desruelle, J., Senatore, G., Fivet, C. (2019). *Structures*, 18, 128–137.
- **What it is:** Computational framework that designs new trusses by selecting from a stock of reclaimed members instead of optimising a single virgin design.
- **Why it matters for the team:** Foundational EPFL SXL paper showing that "form follows availability" can lower embodied carbon while still satisfying structural performance. Direct precedent for reuse as a designed (not improvised) method.
- **What to reuse:** The stock-constrained design logic — useful as a one-line caption for a reclaimed-brick tile in the installation.
- **Limitations:** Trusses, not masonry; assumes a curated stock.

### Küpfer, Bastien-Masse & Fivet, 2019 onward — Form Follows Availability / Reuse of Cut Concrete (SXL programme)
- **Tier:** Peer-reviewed + lab grey literature (sxl.epfl.ch/research/reuse-of-concrete).
- **Citation:** EPFL SXL research line on reuse of cut concrete; see project page and JIASS paper "Form Follows Availability – Designing Structures Through Reuse" (Ingenta).
- **What it is:** Programme of cutting load-bearing concrete from buildings under demolition and reassembling it as new structural elements.
- **Why it matters for the team:** This is the parent research line behind Re:Crete, framing reused concrete as a viable structural material category, not salvage.
- **What to reuse:** Diagrams of saw-cut concrete blocks and stock catalogues — visually legible at tabletop scale.
- **Limitations:** Concrete-specific; brick reuse logic is similar but not identical (mortar bonding vs. cut blocks).

### De Wolf, Hoxha & Fivet, 2020 — Comparison of Environmental Assessment Methods When Reusing Building Components
- **Tier:** Peer-reviewed (*Sustainable Cities and Society*, Vol. 61, 2020).
- **Citation:** De Wolf, C., Hoxha, E., Fivet, C. (2020). *Sustainable Cities and Society*, 61, 102322.
- **What it is:** Case-study comparison of LCA methods applied to a reuse project (Kopfbau Halle 118, Winterthur) showing that different allocation rules produce wide-ranging results and that "embedded use value, versatility, and storage impacts" are poorly captured.
- **Why it matters for the team:** This is the key paper to cite if the peer reviewer says "reuse beats 3DP on every LCA metric." The honest answer is: yes on Module A1–A3 (production) under standard cut-off rules, but allocation choices matter. Defends the political point with rigour.
- **What to reuse:** The methodological caution. Avoid over-claiming.
- **Limitations:** Methods debate is unresolved across EN 15804 versions.

### EPFL SXL, 2021 — Re:Crete Footbridge
- **Tier:** Built prototype + lab page + press (actu.epfl.ch; ribaj.com; construction21.org).
- **Citation:** Küpfer, C., Bastien-Masse, M., Devènes, J., Fivet, C. EPFL SXL & Smart Living Lab, Fribourg, 2021.
- **What it is:** 10 m post-tensioned segmented arch built from 25 saw-cut concrete blocks recovered from a demolition site.
- **Why it matters for the team:** Headline number from the comparative LCA: same GHG emissions as a glulam arch of equivalent span, ~one third the CO2 of an equivalent new reinforced-concrete arch. This is the cleanest peer-of-peers comparison they can cite to a peer reviewer ("reuse can beat timber, not just new concrete").
- **What to reuse:** The arch-of-blocks geometry maps cleanly to a tabletop demonstrator; the LCA bar chart is widely reproduced.
- **Limitations:** One-off prototype; no service-life data yet; arch geometry forced by structural compatibility of the cut blocks.

### Küpfer, Bastien-Masse & Fivet, 2023 — Reuse of Cut Concrete (Journal article)
- **Tier:** Peer-reviewed (*Journal of Cleaner Production* / *Resources, Conservation & Recycling* line — verify exact venue).
- **Citation:** Küpfer, C. et al. (2023) — exact DOI to verify against EPFL Infoscience. [unverified — confirm venue and year]
- **What it is:** Systematic life-cycle accounting for the saw-cut-and-reuse process including cutting energy, transport, and post-tensioning hardware.
- **Why it matters for the team:** Provides the "warts-and-all" embodied-carbon number that includes the energy of cutting — essential for an honest LCA card.
- **Limitations:** Citation requires verification before use.

### Stockhammer (ed.), 2020 — Upcycling: Reuse and Repurposing as a Design Principle in Architecture
- **Tier:** Edited academic volume (Triest Verlag / Park Books distribution; uni.li publication record).
- **Citation:** Stockhammer, D. (Ed.) (2020). *Upcycling: Reuse and Repurposing as a Design Principle in Architecture.* University of Liechtenstein.
- **What it is:** Catalogue + essays from the University of Liechtenstein research group on Built Heritage & Circular Construction. Not the literal "Don't Waste a Thing!" title the team cited, but the closest verifiable counterpart.
- **Why it matters for the team:** Stockhammer's framing — "borrow rather than dispose, building stock as material bank" — gives the installation its rhetorical spine without falling into purely technical LCA debates.
- **What to reuse:** The "inventory before design" principle as a wall-text quote.
- **Limitations:** German-language scholarship dominates; some essays are not peer-reviewed.

### baubüro in situ, 2021 — K.118 Kopfbau Halle 118, Winterthur
- **Tier:** Built work + practitioner documentation (insitu.ch; ArchDaily; TU Delft Circular Design Atlas).
- **Citation:** baubüro in situ, K.118 Kopfbau Halle 118, Winterthur (2021).
- **What it is:** Three-storey extension on a former Sulzer hall built almost entirely from reclaimed components — load-bearing steel from a Basel skeleton, façade panels from a Zurich office, etc.
- **Why it matters for the team:** Practitioner counter-evidence to the claim that reuse only works at pavilion scale. Reported ~60% reduction in GHG and 500 t of primary materials saved versus a new-build baseline.
- **What to reuse:** The component-provenance map (every part labelled with its source building) — a powerful tabletop graphic.
- **Limitations:** Self-reported figures; the EPFL "difficult interpretation of LCA" critique applies.

### Madaster (Rau) & BAMB consortium, 2015–2020 — Material Passports
- **Tier:** Practitioner/policy (madaster.com; bamb2020.eu; GlobalABC best-practice brief).
- **Citation:** BAMB (Buildings as Material Banks), EU Horizon 2020 grant agreement 642384, 2015–2019; Madaster Foundation, NL.
- **What it is:** Digital infrastructure (BAMB pilot generated 300+ passports; Madaster operationalised the registry) that makes building stock tradable as future material supply.
- **Why it matters for the team:** This is the system-level enabler. Without passports, reuse stays artisanal. The installation can show the gap between "stock exists" and "stock is findable."
- **Limitations:** Policy uptake is uneven; no binding EU mandate yet (Digital Product Passport under ESPR is the closest analogue).

### Concular / Restado, 2020– — Marketplace for reclaimed building materials
- **Tier:** Practitioner platform (restado.de; concular.de; European Circular Economy Stakeholder Platform best-practice entry).
- **What it is:** Largest European marketplace for reclaimed construction materials, plus AI-driven matching of demolition supply to new-build demand; operates Urban Mining Hub interim storage in Berlin.
- **Why it matters for the team:** Concrete (sorry) evidence that the market layer exists, not just the policy layer. A QR code on the installation could link to a live Restado listing as a provocation.

### Settimi, Gamerro & Weinand, EPFL IBOIS, ongoing — Augmented Carpentry
- **Tier:** PhD research + open-source software (epfl.ch/labs/ibois/augmented-carpentry; github.com/ibois-epfl).
- **Citation:** Settimi, A., Gamerro, J., Weinand, Y. EPFL IBOIS / Laboratory for Timber Constructions.
- **What it is:** AR-guided handheld toolchain enabling sub-millimetre carpentry on irregular timber, designed to make non-standard (including reclaimed) members tractable.
- **Why it matters for the team:** The robotics bridge between "reclaimed = artisanal" and "reclaimed = computationally addressable." Relevant if the team frames their installation around the MRAC robotics angle.
- **Limitations:** This is IBOIS, not strictly EPFL CRCL. The team's brief conflates them — flag in their report. CRCL's "Discrete Wood" research line was not cleanly retrievable in this search; recommend a direct query to crcl.epfl.ch.

### Pomponi & Moncaster, 2016 — Embodied carbon mitigation and reduction in the built environment
- **Tier:** Peer-reviewed (*Journal of Environmental Management*, 181, 687–700).
- **Citation:** Pomponi, F., Moncaster, A. (2016). *Journal of Environmental Management*, 181, 687–700.
- **What it is:** Systematic review identifying 17 mitigation strategies; concludes no single strategy suffices but reuse of carbon-intensive materials is consistently among the most effective.
- **Why it matters for the team:** The general-claim citation. When the team writes "reuse beats new production on embodied carbon," this is the underwriting source.

### Mensah-Attipoe et al., MDPI 2025 — Recycled Components in 3D Concrete Printing Mixes: A Review
- **Tier:** Peer-reviewed review (*Materials*, 18(19), 4517, 2025).
- **What it is:** Scientometric review of recycled CDW (RCA, RFA, crushed brick, glass) in 3DCP mixes.
- **Why it matters for the team:** This is the bridging case. Combining 3DP with reclaimed aggregate is an active research front; LCA studies report up to 48% CO2 reduction at component level when cement is reduced and recycled fines are used.
- **Limitations:** Most reductions come from cement substitution, not the recycled aggregate itself; transport can erase gains. The reviewed studies note "lack of comprehensive guidelines and standards."

### Liu et al., 2025 — LCA of prefabricated 3D-printed structural units using recycled CDW aggregates (China case)
- **Tier:** Peer-reviewed (*Journal of Building Engineering / Energy & Buildings*, 2025 — confirm).
- **What it is:** Cradle-to-gate LCA of a real prefabricated 3DP unit cast with recycled aggregate.
- **Why it matters for the team:** Closes the loop between methods 2 and 4 — useful if the team wants to argue reuse-vs-3DP is a false dichotomy and the strongest position is "reuse first, 3DP-with-recycled-aggregate where reuse fails."

## The "4th method" question: should reclaimed brick be added?

**The literature supports adding it, but reframes the question.**

The peer-reviewed evidence is consistent: reusing structural components avoids the bulk of cradle-to-gate emissions (Modules A1–A3), which dominate embodied carbon for masonry and concrete. Re:Crete's measured ~1/3 emissions vs. new RC, and Halle 118's reported ~60% GHG reduction at building scale, are the two strongest single-project data points the team can cite. The Pomponi & Moncaster review and the De Wolf/Hoxha/Fivet methods paper underwrite the general claim.

So on embodied-carbon-per-functional-unit, reclaimed brick will almost certainly outperform 3DP concrete and likely also outperform 3DP earth on cradle-to-gate, because (a) bricks are already fired (the energy debt is sunk and amortised over a second life cycle), and (b) 3DP concrete still requires high-OPC mixes for printability. The peer reviewer is broadly right.

**However**, three caveats from the literature push against a naïve "reuse wins everything" frame:

1. **Allocation rules matter.** De Wolf et al. (2020) show LCA results swing widely depending on whether the original production burden is allocated to the first or second life. The team should display this explicitly rather than picking the most flattering number.
2. **Use phase and end-of-life are usually omitted.** The reuse advantage is concentrated in A1–A3; if the comparator (3DP earth) has lower operational and demolition impacts, full-LCA gaps narrow.
3. **Stock availability is geographic and political.** Catalonia/Spain's Law 7/2022 sets a 70%-by-weight CDW recovery target, but reuse (vs. recycling-down) remains a small fraction. The installation's political point is sharper if it shows what stock *exists* in Barcelona than if it just cites Swiss prototypes.

**Recommendation:** add reclaimed brick as the 4th method, but stage it as the "control case" against which the other three are measured — not as a fourth competitor. This makes the political point ("we already have the materials; the question is why we don't use them") without forcing the team to prove reuse is best on every axis.

## Cross-cutting themes

- **Stock-constrained design.** Brütting, Fivet, Stockhammer all converge on inverting the design pipeline: inventory first, design after.
- **The methods debate is unresolved.** EN 15804 cut-off vs. avoided-burden allocation produces different reuse advantages.
- **Robotics is the unlock for non-standard stock.** Augmented Carpentry, SXL's stock-matching algorithms, and Discrete Wood-type work all address the "reclaimed = irregular = expensive to handle" bottleneck.
- **Material passports are the missing infrastructure.** Without them, every reuse project re-does its own inventory.

## Catalonia / EU regulatory context

- **EU CDW framework:** Waste Framework Directive 2008/98/EC sets a 70% recovery target for non-hazardous CDW. The EU CDW Management Protocol (2018) promotes selective demolition.
- **Spain Law 7/2022** (Waste and Contaminated Soils for a Circular Economy) transposes EU obligations and sets the 70%-by-weight target for CDW reuse/recycling/recovery.
- **Catalonia PRECAT20** (Programa general de prevenció i gestió de residus) prioritises reuse preparation over recycling and operates the (De)construct for Circular Economy training programme through the Generalitat's environmental quality directorate.
- **Digital Product Passport (ESPR, 2024):** EU-wide passports for construction products are scheduled for delegated acts in 2027–2030. Madaster and BAMB pilots are the de facto reference.

## What's missing in this literature

- **Tabletop / pedagogical-scale comparative LCAs.** Almost all evidence is from full buildings or pavilions; the team's installation is itself a contribution if it makes the comparison legible at hand-scale.
- **Reuse + 3DP combined LCAs at building scale.** The MDPI reviews are mix-design level, not building level.
- **Catalan-specific reuse stock data.** PRECAT20 reports on tonnages but not on what is actually re-installed vs. downcycled.
- **CRCL "Discrete Wood" specifically:** the team's brief names it but the search did not return clear publications under that exact name. Recommend a direct check at crcl.epfl.ch and the lab's publication list.

## Direct recommendations for the team

1. **Add reclaimed brick as a 4th, framed-as-baseline method.** Show LCA bars side-by-side with the existing three. Use Re:Crete's 1/3-of-RC and Halle 118's 60% reduction as anchor numbers, both with citations.
2. **Cite De Wolf, Hoxha & Fivet (2020) explicitly** when presenting the LCA — it shields the team from the "you cherry-picked the allocation" critique.
3. **Pair the brick method with a robotic-handling reference** (Augmented Carpentry / SXL stock-matching) so it sits inside the MRAC robotics frame and not as a "low-tech protest" tile.
4. **Anchor the political point in Catalan stock**, not Swiss prototypes. Pull a PRECAT20 figure or a Concular-equivalent Catalan platform listing if one exists.
5. **For the bridging tile, consider a "3DP with recycled CDW aggregate" callout** rather than a full method — it shows the team understands the methods are not mutually exclusive.
6. **Verify the Küpfer 2023 venue and DOI** before citing in any submitted document.
