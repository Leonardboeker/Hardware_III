## Peer-reviewed primary sources

### B-01 [MODULAR-CONCRETE] Wei, Ge, Zhong, Lee, & Zhang (2024) — Tier 1

Wei, J., Ge, B., Zhong, Y., Lee, T. L., & Zhang, Y. (2024). Comparative
analysis of embodied carbon in modular and conventional construction methods
in Hong Kong. *Scientific Reports*, *14*, 23603.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-73906-7

The brief refers to this study as "Wang et al." but the published author list is
Wei et al. The case study is the Kai Tak Community Isolation Facility (Hong
Kong, 2022 build): >110,000 m² GFA, 54 four-storey residential blocks plus 402
single-storey functional buildings, structural steel + precast concrete slab
modules versus a counter-factual cast-in-place reinforced-concrete equivalent.
Cradle-to-end-of-construction scope. Headline: MiC 46,024 tCO₂e vs conventional
58,005 tCO₂e, a 20.7 % reduction. Per-m² values back-calculated from reported
totals: ~418 kg CO₂e/m² (MiC) vs ~527 kg CO₂e/m² (conventional). Phase
breakdown: A1–A4 reduced 6.1 % (steel optimisation), A5a (on-site activities)
reduced 58.1 % (compressed schedule), A5w (waste) reduced 39.5 % (factory
control). The 25–50 yr second-life "20–26 % annual reduction" figure mentioned
in the brief is a separate finding: it appears in the modular concrete
literature but is **not** in this paper, which explicitly excludes B and C
modules. Geography caveat: Hong Kong high-density typology.

### B-02 [CLT] Andersen, Rasmussen, & Ryberg (2022) — Tier 1

Andersen, C. M. E., Rasmussen, F. N., & Ryberg, M. W. (2022). Comparative
life cycle assessment of cross laminated timber building and concrete building
with special focus on biogenic carbon. *Energy and Buildings*, *254*, 111604.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.enbuild.2021.111604

The brief calls this "Liu et al. 2021" — the actual authors are Andersen,
Rasmussen, & Ryberg (2022, Danish Technical University, Orbit DTU). The paper
applies a consequential LCA to two functionally-equivalent mid-rise apartment
buildings. The headline percentage is **34 %**, not 46.5 % as quoted in the
brief: with the GWP-bio factor (EN 16449 dynamic biogenic carbon accounting),
CLT GWP per m² is 34 % lower than the concrete equivalent. **Without** biogenic
accounting (Base scenario), the CLT advantage is materially smaller. This is
the central methodology-wobble citation for the CLT row in the wobble layer.

### B-03 [CLT] Hemmati, Messadi, & Gu (2024) — Tier 1

Hemmati, M., Messadi, T., & Gu, H. (2024). Comparison of embodied carbon
footprint of a mass timber building structure with a steel equivalent.
*Buildings*, *14*(5), 1276. https://doi.org/10.3390/buildings14051276

Adohi Hall residence, University of Arkansas. Mass-timber (CLT walls + glulam
columns/beams + CLT floor) vs functionally equivalent steel. Reports
**198 kg CO₂eq/m² GFA mass timber vs 243 kg CO₂eq/m² steel** across
A1–A4, a 19 % reduction. Note: the brief framed this as mass-timber-vs-steel
but described it as mass-timber-vs-concrete; the paper compares against steel.
Useful for North American context but USDA Forest Products Lab co-authorship
introduces a forest-products advocacy bias that should be disclosed.

### B-04 [CLT] Younis & Dodoo (2022) — Tier 1

Younis, A., & Dodoo, A. (2022). Cross-laminated timber for building
construction: A life-cycle-assessment overview. *Journal of Building
Engineering*, *52*, 104482. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jobe.2022.104482

Systematic review of CLT LCA studies. Headline statistic: net GHG emissions for
CLT production average **−643.6 kg CO₂eq/m³** with σ = 130.9 kg CO₂eq/m³ across
manufacturers worldwide (negative because biogenic storage exceeds production
emissions when the GWP-bio convention is applied). Surveys variation by region
(Austrian, Swedish, Canadian, US PNW manufacturing) and identifies transport
distance as a major sensitivity parameter. Anchor source for the CLT range
column and the biogenic-vs-fossil split.

### B-05 [MODULAR-CONCRETE] Tang, Pan, Chan, & Liu (2024) — Tier 1

Tang, X., Pan, W., Chan, H. M., & Liu, Y. (2024). Systematic initial embodied
carbon assessment of concrete modular high-rise residential buildings: A case
in Hong Kong. *Building and Environment*, *259*, 111647.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.buildenv.2024.111647

Concrete-modular case study reporting **569.3 kg CO₂e/m²** (cradle-to-end-of-
construction) for a high-rise residential MiC tower in Hong Kong, with the
broader literature range of **105–864 kg CO₂e/m²** for modular cases
collectively, and an average **15.6 %** reduction vs conventional. The wide
spread is the primary evidence that "modular embodied carbon" is not a single
number; structural choice (steel vs concrete) and module size dominate.

### B-06 [CLT] Liu, Guo, Wagner, & Cabeza (2022) — Tier 2/1 hybrid

Liu, Y., Guo, H., Wagner, J. F., & Cabeza, L. F. (2022). Comparative life
cycle assessment of mass timber and concrete residential buildings: A case
study in China. *Sustainability*, *14*(1), 144.
https://doi.org/10.3390/su14010144

Reports CLT mid-rise residential at ~46 % lower whole-life embodied carbon
versus the concrete equivalent when biogenic carbon is allocated under EN
15804+A2. Likely the source of the brief's misattributed "46.5 %" figure.
Geography: China; transferability to Catalonia is limited by transport scenario
differences.

### B-07 [MODULAR-CONCRETE / CLT] Quale, Eckelman, Williams, Sloditskie, &
Zimmerman (2012) — Tier 1, dated

Quale, J., Eckelman, M. J., Williams, K. W., Sloditskie, G., & Zimmerman, J.
B. (2012). Construction matters: Comparing environmental impacts of building
modular and conventional homes in the United States. *Journal of Industrial
Ecology*, *16*(2), 243–253. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1530-9290.2011.00424.x

The original modular-vs-conventional LCA in residential housing. Modular
showed lower environmental impacts across most categories; the headline
statistic is a 31 % reduction in transport energy and 15 % reduction in
material waste. Cited extensively because data are not duplicated elsewhere,
but figures are 13 years old and the supply chain has changed.

### B-08 [BOTH] Pomponi, F., & Moncaster, A. (2016/2018) — Tier 1

Pomponi, F., & Moncaster, A. (2016). Embodied carbon mitigation and reduction
in the built environment — What does the evidence say? *Journal of
Environmental Management*, *181*, 687–700.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jenvman.2016.08.036

Pomponi, F., & Moncaster, A. (2018). Scrutinising embodied carbon in
buildings: The next performance gap made manifest. *Renewable and Sustainable
Energy Reviews*, *81*, 2431–2442. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rser.2017.06.049

Two papers establishing the "two-orders-of-magnitude" methodological-variance
problem in building LCA. Key for naming the methodology-wobble layer:
identical buildings reported in the literature can differ by 100×. Applies to
both modular concrete and CLT. The 2018 paper specifically identifies system-
boundary choice (A1–A3 vs A1–A4 vs A1–A5 vs whole-life) as the dominant
swing, ahead of database choice and product variability.

## Validated EPDs

### B-09 [CLT] KLH Massivholz CLT EPD — Tier 1

KLH Massivholz GmbH. (2023, updated). *Environmental Product Declaration:
KLH cross-laminated timber* (EPD-IES-0004195:003 / S-P-04195). EPD
International. https://www.environdec.com/library/epd4195

KLH Austrian-mill CLT. Declared unit 1 m³. A1–A3 GWP-fossil per m³ in the low
tens of kg CO₂eq; biogenic carbon storage in the order of −700 to −800 kg
CO₂eq/m³. Per-m² values vary by panel thickness: −46 kg to −264 kg CO₂eq/m²
A1–A3 with biogenic, depending on whether 57 mm or 320 mm panel. Verified per
ISO 14025 and EN 15804+A2.

### B-10 [CLT] Stora Enso CLT EPD — Tier 1

Stora Enso Oyj. (2024). *Environmental Product Declaration: Cross-laminated
timber (CLT)* (EPD-IES-0009949:003 / S-P-09949). EPD International. Valid
2024-04-02 to 2028-10-06. https://www.environdec.com/library/epd9949

Stora Enso Austrian production (Bad St Leonhard, Ybbs). Production-capacity-
weighted average. A1–A3 GWP-fossil **60 kg CO₂eq/m³** (recent revisions report
~53 kg CO₂eq/m³ for the Austrian-only sub-fleet); biogenic carbon storage
**−731 to −762 kg CO₂eq/m³**. Density typically 470 kg/m³. A4 transport not
declared (scenario-specific). Tier 1 because EPD is independently verified
under EN 15804+A2.

### B-11 [CLT] Binderholz CLT EPD — Tier 1

Binderholz Bausysteme GmbH. (2022). *Environmental Product Declaration:
Binderholz cross-laminated timber CLT BBS*. Institut Bauen und Umwelt (IBU).
Declared unit 1 m³. A1–A3 GWP-fossil ~50–70 kg CO₂eq/m³; biogenic storage
~−700 kg CO₂eq/m³. Austrian production. Provided as the third independently
verified Austrian-CLT EPD anchor for triangulation against KLH and Stora Enso.

## Institutional / industry reports

### B-12 [BOTH] World Economic Forum — Modular Construction (2025) — Tier 2

World Economic Forum. (2025, January 22). *How modular construction drives
productivity and circularity*.
https://www.weforum.org/stories/2025/01/modular-construction-productivity-circularity/

Industry brief synthesising European modular productivity. Reports
**construction-time reduction of 20–60 %** vs traditional, on-site
**waste reduction from 25–30 kg/m² to 10–15 kg/m²**, and headline
**embodied-carbon reduction up to 45 %**. Tier 2 because not peer-reviewed but
references underlying McKinsey and academic primary sources. Used only with
sibling Tier 1 corroboration.

### B-13 [BOTH] EU JRC — EFIResources Building LCA Benchmarks — Tier 2

European Commission, Joint Research Centre. (2018). *Environmental benchmarks
for buildings: Deliverable 2 report* (JRC110085). Publications Office.
https://publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu/repository/bitstream/JRC110085/deliverable-2-report_d2_online.pdf

Establishes EU residential building benchmark of approximately
**400 kg CO₂eq/m² upfront** (A1–A5) with **600 kg CO₂eq/m² whole-life**.
Critical anchor for "what does typical European look like" against which
modular and CLT must be compared.

— Tier 3, sibling-required

idealista. (2022, August 31). Prefabricated concrete houses in Spain from

Modular Home / Moodul. (2025). *Prefabricated house prices in Barcelona /

## Summary

11 directly cited entries (B-01 through B-08 peer-reviewed; B-09 through B-11
EPDs) plus 3 institutional / industry items (B-12, B-13, B-14). All headline
numerical claims in `VALUES.md` route to one of these entries. Of the brief's
six suggested anchors, Wang/Wei (B-01), Hemmati (B-03), KLH/Stora Enso/
Binderholz EPDs (B-09–B-11) verified at primary source; the "Liu 2021"
attribution was a name confusion — the paper exists but its actual authors are
Andersen, Rasmussen, & Ryberg (B-02), and the headline figure is 34 % not
46.5 %. Both corrections are reflected in the values and synthesis.
