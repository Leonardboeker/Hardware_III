# Narrative Implications — Sub-strand 9 and the S1 Political-Dimension Critique

The S1 peer-review critique flagged a missing political dimension: _who loses jobs in each method?_ This document translates the FTE / skill-mix data from `VALUES.md` Table 9 into narrative guidance for the installation's projection layer. It is structured around the four asks in the brief: (a) what the actual FTE-per-100m² data says; (b) skill-level shifts; (c) Catalonia-specific context; (d) what the projection layer should and should not say to a child visitor versus a decision-maker.

---

## (a) What the FTE-per-100m² data actually says

Method-by-method, anchored to the bands in `VALUES.md` Table 9:

- **Masonry (M1).** ~0.30-0.50 FTE/100m². 100 % onsite. Conventional bricklayer + labourer + mason crew. The reference Spanish productivity benchmark (CaixaBank: ~€112 400 GVA per FTE per year) is consistent with a small Catalan masonry crew producing ~200-300 m² per FTE-year.
- **3D-printed concrete (M2-C).** ~0.10-0.20 FTE/100m². ~70 % onsite (printer operation + finishing) + ~30 % offsite (mix prep, BIM, programming, machine maintenance). Roughly _one-third to one-half_ the FTE of masonry.
- **3D-printed earth (M2-E).** ~0.10-0.25 FTE/100m². The local-material advantage means more onsite (~80 %) and very little offsite, but at the same FTE band as M2-C.
- **Modular concrete (M3-C).** ~0.20-0.35 FTE/100m². 20-25 % onsite / 75-80 % factory. The factory FTE is _real labour_ but it is not Catalan onsite labour.
- **Modular CLT (M3-T).** ~0.20-0.35 FTE/100m². 20-30 % onsite / 70-80 % factory. Most factory FTE is in Austria or Germany.
- **Reclaimed brick (M4).** ~0.50-0.90 FTE/100m². 100 % onsite. _More_ FTE than masonry, because deconstruction yields below 40 % require labour both at the source and the new site.

The headline ranking is: **reclaimed brick > masonry > modular > 3D-printed**, with 3D-printed using roughly _one-fifth to one-half_ the labour of masonry.

A second-order observation: _onsite_ FTE per 100m² shrinks much faster than total FTE under prefab. M3-C/M3-T use 0.20-0.35 FTE total but only 0.05-0.10 _onsite_ — a 60-80 % onsite-FTE reduction. The total productivity gain is 30-40 %; the local-onsite-jobs reduction is 60-80 %. This gap is the political payload.

The bands should be displayed with explicit ±50 % wobble. Spanish-construction FTE-per-m² is genuinely under-studied at peer-review precision. The honest projection looks like a fuzzy bar, not a ruler.

## (b) Skill-level shifts: 3DPC and prefab move work, but to whom?

The cleanest way to read the skill-mix shift is by tracing where the value-added work goes.

**Masonry → 3DPC.** A Catalan-trade-trained mason has a five-to-seven-year apprenticeship arc (oficial qualification). They are replaced by: (i) a printer-operator-technician (electromechanical training, ~2 years); (ii) a BIM modeller / programmer (engineering or architecture qualification, ~5 years university); (iii) a mix-rheology technician (chemistry / materials qualification). The new roles are _credential-different_, not just skill-different. The mason cannot retrain into the BIM modeller within a project cycle.

**Masonry → modular.** The mason is replaced by a factory operator (Austrian or Polish), a CNC operator, and a crane / assembly crew on site. The factory work is _arguably more skilled_ in the sense that it requires programmed-machinery competence; it is _less skilled_ in the sense that it requires less spatial and material-judgement craft. The on-site crane crew is small and highly skilled.

**Masonry → reclaimed brick.** The mason is _more in demand_, plus deconstruction crew, plus brick-cleaning crew (low-skill but not unskilled — Salmio & Huuhka 2026 report 1.4 hours per worker per re-usable brick yield with hand-held tools). Reclaimed brick is the labour-positive method.

The brief asks the load-bearing question: _is the new labour available to the same people?_ The empirical answer is **no** for two structural reasons:

1. **Geography.** Modular factory labour is in Austria, Germany, Poland, occasionally Spain. Catalan onsite labour cannot commute. 3DPC factory labour is more dispersed but still requires industrial-zone proximity, not residential-construction-site proximity.
2. **Credential.** The mason-to-BIM-modeller and mason-to-CNC-operator transitions require formal-education credentials that the typical 50-year-old Catalan mason does not have and is not subsidised to acquire. ILO 2018 explicitly flags this: low-skill on-site labour (often immigrant, often informally contracted) bears the brunt of automation displacement, while the new tech-and-engineering FTE accrues to a different cohort.

The installation should not pretend this transition is neutral. McKinsey's 2019 "20-25 % labour-cost reduction" is true at the project ledger; it is _not_ a 20-25 % "fewer jobs" claim, because much of that cost reduction comes from substituting Polish factory hourly rates for Catalan unionised onsite rates. That substitution _is_ the political dimension.

## (c) Catalonia-specific context

Three pieces of Spanish / Catalan context anchor the framing.

**Post-2008 recovery patterns.** The Spanish construction sector lost ~1.7 million workers from 2008 (peak ~2.7 M) to 2014 (trough ~1.0 M). The recovery to 2024 is partial — current sector employment is still well below 2008 peak. Construction-sector unemployment in 2024 Q1 (INE) was 14.8 %, vs 13.1 % national average. The sector is _structurally under-employed_, not labour-scarce.

**The productivity narrative.** BBVA Research and CaixaBank have published prominent analyses arguing Spanish construction labour productivity has dropped >20 % since 2013. This is the dominant frame in Spanish-business media and in MICA / Foment-style industry-association communications: "the sector needs more automation". From the bank-research angle this is true. From the labour-political angle it is the framing under which automation is sold and the displacement is rationalised.

**Catalan industry capacity.** Catalonia's logistics and modular-industry capacity is uneven. There is no major Catalan concrete-MiC supplier dominating the local market (cf. Hong Kong's CIMC). CLT is overwhelmingly imported from Austria. The Hispalyt-network brick industry _is_ Catalan-and-Spanish (Cerámica Pierola, Malpesa, La Covadonga, etc.). The 3DPC space in Catalonia has IAAC's Valldaura Labs as the demonstrated R&D anchor but no permitted residential dwellings as of May 2026.

So the Catalonia-specific reading of the four methods on labour:

- **Masonry**: keeps work in the local economy. High onsite FTE. Hispalyt-aligned. Politically the path-of-least-resistance for unions.
- **3DPC**: reduces local FTE. New FTE (BIM, R&D, materials science) does accrue to Catalan institutions like IAAC, but at a small scale.
- **Modular concrete**: moves most labour out of Catalonia (likely imports). Net job-loss for the Catalan onsite sector.
- **Modular CLT**: moves labour to Austria / Germany. Catalan onsite gains assembly + crane crew, loses bricklayers.
- **Reclaimed brick**: keeps and grows local labour. Politically aligned with circular-economy and labour-protective frames.

This ordering — reclaimed > masonry > 3DPC > modular — is _opposite_ to the typical embodied-carbon ordering that the installation will also display. That contradiction is the most honest single visualisation the installation can offer.

## (d) What to say to whom

The brief asks for guidance on what the projection should and should not say to a child visitor versus a decision-maker. The answer differs by audience because the political payload is large and the data is uncertain.

### To a child visitor

_Show, don't tell._ A child visitor traces a footprint, sets a height, picks a method, and watches an animation. The jobs layer for them should be:

- **A small icon-stack representation:** "this method needs ~5 people on-site for ~3 weeks" vs "this method needs ~2 people on-site for ~1 week + ~3 people in a factory in Austria for ~2 weeks". Pictographic, not numeric.
- **Geographic colouring:** if labour is local (masonry, reclaimed brick, 3DP-earth) the workers icon is one colour; if labour is regional / imported the icons are another colour and a small map shows where.
- **No moralising.** The visualisation should not editorialise that one method is "better"; it should make the trade-off visible and let the visitor sit with it.

What the projection should NOT do for a child:

- Display point estimates without bands. Children read points as facts.
- Imply that the bricklayer who lost their job has a new job in the factory. They probably do not.
- Display absolute FTE numbers without temporal context. "0.30 FTE/100m²" is meaningless to a 9-year-old; "5 people for 3 weeks" is meaningful.

### To a decision-maker (architect, public-procurement officer, planner)

_Show the wobble explicitly and own the trade-off._ The decision-maker's projection should include:

- **The full 0.10-0.90 FTE/100m² band, with method-specific sub-bands and ±50 % wobble bars.**
- **The on-site / factory split as a stacked bar, separately from total.** Total FTE is a project-cost metric; on-site FTE is a local-jobs metric. They should not be summed visually.
- **The skill-mix shift as a Sankey-style flow:** "masonry: skilled-mason 30 %, semi-skilled labourer 40 %, unskilled labourer 30 %" → "3DPC: technician-engineer 50 %, semi-skilled finishing 30 %, unskilled 20 %" + a line connecting the categories. Make it visually explicit that the categories don't map cleanly.
- **Source-bias direction inline.** "Industry sources estimate lower displacement; advocacy sources estimate higher." This is a fact about the data, not a hedge.
- **The Catalonia-context box:** post-2008 recovery, current 14.8 % construction unemployment, Hispalyt's Catalan footprint, CLT's Austrian dependency. The decision-maker should not have to know this to read the data; the projection should put it next to the numbers.
- **The Salmio & Huuhka 2026 reclaimed-brick threshold:** carbon-positive up to 480 km T1 / 315 km T2. This single peer-reviewed datum reframes the entire reclaimed-brick option from "supply-constrained novelty" to "active labour-and-carbon double dividend within a 480 km radius".

What the projection should NOT do for a decision-maker:

- Display McKinsey or WEF figures without the explicit bias-direction tag. "Industry-favourable" is a fact, not an opinion.
- Pretend that 3DPC or modular displacement is a wash. It is not. The labour _moves_, not just _shrinks_, and where it moves matters.
- Hide the asymmetry of evidence quality. Masonry data is well-grounded; 3D-printed-earth data is essentially absent at peer-review precision. The wobble layer must be _wider_ for the less-evidenced methods.

---

## Closing note for the narrative owner

The honest narrative is this: the four methods sit on a frontier where carbon and labour are in tension, and the cheapest-carbon method (3D-printed earth) is also the most-experimental and the least-empirically-grounded for labour effects, while the most-labour-positive method (reclaimed brick) is supply-constrained but, per Salmio & Huuhka 2026, carbon-competitive up to 480 km. Masonry is the comfortable middle: known on every axis, mediocre on every axis. Modular is fast and high-quality but moves jobs out of Catalonia.

The installation's job is not to recommend a method. It is to make the trade-offs visible. Sub-strand 9 is the strand that, more than any other, requires the visitor to sit with discomfort. The projection layer should make that discomfort precise.
