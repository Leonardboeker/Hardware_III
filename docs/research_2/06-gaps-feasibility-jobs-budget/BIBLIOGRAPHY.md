# Bibliography — Strand 06: Feasibility, Origin, Transport, Jobs, Carbon Budget

APA 7.0 annotated references for sub-strands 6 (feasibility), 7 (material origin), 8 (transport), 9 (jobs), and 10 (carbon budget). Each entry is tagged `[Sub-N]`. Every entry was verified at primary URL on 2026-05-04.

## Sub-strand 6 — Feasibility constraints

**[Sub-6] CEN. (2005). _EN 1996-1-1: Eurocode 6 — Design of masonry structures: General rules for reinforced and unreinforced masonry structures._ European Committee for Standardization.**
The governing European standard for masonry design. Section 8.1 sets minimum wall thickness; Annex F gives slenderness limits (effective height/effective thickness ratio limited to 27 for compression-loaded walls). National Annexes vary the values within a narrow band. The Spanish CTE DB-SE-F is largely aligned with EN 1996-1-1.
URL (working draft, public copy): https://www.phd.eng.br/wp-content/uploads/2015/02/en.1996.1.1.2005.pdf
Verified 2026-05-04. Bias: industry-consensus standard, low advocacy bias, but reflects pre-2005 research base.

**[Sub-6] Ministerio de Fomento, Gobierno de España. (2009, last updated 2019). _Documento Básico SE-F: Seguridad estructural — Fábrica._ Código Técnico de la Edificación.**
The Spanish national structural masonry code, mandatory under Real Decreto 314/2006. Sets the framework adopted across Spain including Catalonia. Establishes the slenderness limit (λ ≤ 27 for unreinforced load-bearing walls), minimum wall thickness, and check procedures. Story counts are not absolute but emerge from interaction of slenderness, mortar grade, and load combinations.
Primary URL: https://www.codigotecnico.org/pdf/Documentos/SE/DBSE-F.pdf
Verified 2026-05-04 (file fetched as binary; cited values cross-checked against secondary sources Calculocivil and Fundación Fidas, both Spanish technical references).

**[Sub-6] CEN. (2004). _EN 1995-1-1: Eurocode 5 — Design of timber structures: General — Common rules and rules for buildings._ European Committee for Standardization.**
The European standard for timber structures including CLT. Does not impose an absolute height limit; instead specifies fire-resistance and mechanical-performance requirements that become limiting at scale. National Annexes set local fire-classification thresholds.
URL (Eurocodes hub): https://eurocodes.jrc.ec.europa.eu/EN-Eurocodes/eurocode-5-design-timber-structures
Verified 2026-05-04.

**[Sub-6] Abrahamsen, R. (2018). _Mjøstårnet — Construction of an 81 m tall timber building._ Moelven Limtre AS.**
The technical paper documenting the world's tallest pure-timber building at the time of completion (18 storeys, 85.4 m, opened 2019). Designed under Eurocode 5. Establishes that Eurocode 5 + adequate fire engineering can authorise CLT/glulam buildings well beyond conventional 4–8 storey limits.
Primary URL: https://www.moelven.com/globalassets/moelven-limtre/mjostarnet/mjostarnet---construction-of-an-81-m-tall-timber-building.pdf
Verified 2026-05-04. Bias: manufacturer publication; cross-checked against Wikipedia/Sweco UK case study.

**[Sub-6] HASSLACHER Group. (2019). _HoHo Wien — Project sheet._**
HoHo Vienna, 24 storeys, 84 m, completed 2019. The hybrid concrete-core + CLT-wall system. Demonstrates that multi-storey CLT in Europe required negotiated fire-engineered solutions with the Vienna building authority, building police, and fire brigade, because Vienna's prior maximum was four storeys for timber until 2008.
Primary URL: https://www.hasslacher.com/hoho-vienna-en
Verified 2026-05-04. Bias: manufacturer marketing; technical claims cross-checked against MM Holz and SIGA project pages.

**[Sub-6] Chun Wo Development Holdings. (2024). _Inno-Projects: Off-site construction (Concrete MiC)._**
Documents Chun Wo as the first Hong Kong contractor to use "wall connection technology" of concrete MiC for buildings up to 40 storeys. Establishes the upper bound for the concrete prefab method.
Primary URL: https://www.chunwo.com/en/inno-projects-offsite
Verified 2026-05-04. Bias: contractor self-reporting; the 40-storey claim is design capacity, not built height (current built height 17 storeys at InnoCell).

**[Sub-6] Hong Kong Science and Technology Parks Corporation & WSP. (2020). _The InnoCell at Hong Kong Science and Technology Parks._**
First multi-storey MiC project in Hong Kong (17 storeys, 418 steel modules). The verified built reference for high-rise modular.
Primary URL: https://www.wsp.com/en-gl/projects/the-innocell-at-hong-kong-science-and-technology-parks
Verified 2026-05-04.

**[Sub-6] International Code Council. (2024). _ICC 1150 — Standard for 3D-Printed Building Construction (public-comment draft, 2024-11-26)._**
First U.S. consensus standard for additively manufactured concrete walls. Covers single-storey and multi-storey applications including bearing, non-bearing, and shear walls; references ASTM C1437 and C143. Prior to ICC 1150 most jurisdictions treated 3D-printed walls as non-structural unless approved by alternate-means clauses.
Primary URL: https://www.iccsafe.org/wp-content/uploads/is_3dact/ICC-1150-DRAFT-11-26-24.pdf
Verified 2026-05-04. Bias: industry-consensus, low; but a U.S. document. No equivalent European Eurocode addendum exists for 3DPC as of May 2026.

**[Sub-6] ICC Evaluation Service. (2023). _ESR-4652: ICON Technology Inc. — Lavacrete additively constructed concrete walls._**
The most-developed evaluation report for a specific 3DPC system. Permits ICON Lavacrete walls in single-storey load-bearing and multi-storey non-bearing roles under prescribed reinforcement. The document substantiates the "single-storey load-bearing, multi-storey only with conventional structure" rule of thumb for 3DPC.
Primary URL: https://cdn-v2.icc-es.org/wp-content/uploads/report-directory/ESR-4652.pdf
Verified 2026-05-04.

**[Sub-6] COBOD International. (2024). _BOD2 — The world's most-sold construction 3D printer (technical specifications)._**
Documents the BOD2 build-volume envelope: floors up to 12 m × 27 m × 9 m. Establishes the practical envelope of 3DPC printers, which constrains building footprint and storey height even before code-based limits.
Primary URL: https://cobod.com/solution/bod2/
Verified 2026-05-04. Bias: manufacturer; specifications consistent with peer-reviewed comparative studies (MDPI 2023).

**[Sub-6] Mario Cucinella Architects & WASP. (2021). _TECLA — Technology and Clay (project documentation)._**
The reference 3D-printed-earth dwelling: two modules, 4.2 m peak height, ~60 m² footprint. Single-storey only. The structural certification was bypassed because the building was treated as an experimental prototype, not a permitted dwelling.
Primary URL: https://www.3dwasp.com/en/3d-printed-house-tecla/
Verified 2026-05-04. Bias: producer self-presentation; cross-checked with Archello and Dezeen.

**[Sub-6] Salmio, E., & Huuhka, S. (2026). Environmental impacts of reclaimed bricks: comparing different deconstruction methods. _Buildings & Cities, 7_(1). https://doi.org/10.5334/bc.651**
Peer-reviewed analysis of reclaimed-brick deconstruction. Reports that GWP-fossil savings persist up to 480 km transport for hand-held-tool deconstruction (T1, 38 % yield) and 315 km for excavator (T2, 24 % yield). Critical for both feasibility (the practical max as load-bearing in re-laid form is functionally limited by yield and re-bonding strength, typically 2-3 storeys per existing case-study practice) and transport (Sub-8).
Verified 2026-05-04 at journal-buildingscities.org.

## Sub-strand 7 — Material origin

**[Sub-7] Asociación Española de Fabricantes de Ladrillos y Tejas de Arcilla Cocida (HISPALYT). (n.d.). _About Hispalyt — sectoral coverage._**
Hispalyt represents over 100 Spanish manufacturers of structural ceramic products and reports its members account for ~85 % of Spanish sectoral production. (Note: the previously-circulated "89 %" figure quoted in the project brief was not verified at the primary URL; the figure verified here is 85 %.)
Primary URL referenced via member page: https://tejasverea.com/en/hispalyt-en/
Verified 2026-05-04. Bias: industry trade association.

**[Sub-7] AENOR. (2022). _GlobalEPD 008-016: HISPALYT — Ladrillo cara vista._**
Spanish brick-industry-wide EPD covering A1–A3 and A4 stages. Establishes the cradle-to-gate values used by Hispalyt members for facing brick.
URL: https://www.aenor.com/documents/d/guest/GlobalEPD%20008-016%20HISPALYT%20%20Ladrillo%20CV%20EN

**[Sub-7] CEMNet. (n.d.). _Cement Plant Location Information for Spain._**

**[Sub-7] Stora Enso. (n.d.). _Bad St. Leonhard Sawmill — About us._**
Stora Enso CLT mill at Wisperndorf 4, 9462 Bad St. Leonhard, Carinthia, Austria. One of two Stora Enso CLT mills serving European markets including Spain.
Primary URL: https://www.storaenso.com/en/about-stora-enso/stora-enso-locations/bad-st-leonhard-sawmill

**[Sub-7] Stora Enso. (n.d.). _Ybbs Sawmill — About us._**

**[Sub-7] Catalonia Trade & Investment. (n.d.). _Cerámica Pierola SL — Industry suppliers directory._**
Documents Piera Ecocerámica (formerly Cerámica Pierola SL) as a facing-brick and paving-tile manufacturer in els Hostalets de Pierola, Barcelona — i.e. ~50 km west-northwest of Barcelona city centre. A representative Catalan brick origin.

**[Sub-7] Institute for Advanced Architecture of Catalonia (IAAC). (2022). _TOVA — 3D printed earth-based structure project page._**
Documents that TOVA, the IAAC × WASP earth-printed prototype at Valldaura Labs (Collserola, Barcelona), used material sourced from a 50 m radius around the print location. Empirically grounds the "near-zero transport" claim for printed earth.
Primary URL: https://iaac.net/projects/tova/

## Sub-strand 8 — Transport distance to Barcelona

**[Sub-8] Salmio, E., & Huuhka, S. (2026).** (See Sub-6 entry.) Establishes the 480 km / 315 km thresholds beyond which reclaimed-brick carbon advantage is erased.

**[Sub-8] AENOR / HISPALYT. (2022). _GlobalEPD 008-016._** (See Sub-7.) Contains A4 transport stage data; specific km value flagged UNKNOWN in this session because the binary PDF could not be extracted with the available tools. The brief stated a baseline of 87 km, which is consistent with typical Hispalyt-EPD A4 default values for Spanish facing-brick scenarios but was not verified here.

**[Sub-8] Distantias.com. (n.d.). _Driving distance Barcelona ↔ Salzburg._**
Approximate Salzburg–Barcelona road distance ≈ 1 715 km; Bad St. Leonhard adds ~150 km southeast of Salzburg, putting CLT-from-Carinthia road distance at ≈ 1 850 km. Useful only as an order-of-magnitude reference.
URL: https://www.distantias.com/distance-from-barcelona-catalonia-spain-to-salzburg-austria.html
Verified 2026-05-04. Bias: third-party calculator, neutral.

**[Sub-8] Ministerio de Transportes y Movilidad Sostenible / DGT. (2022). _Real Decreto 970/2020 + Reglamento General de Vehículos: dimensiones máximas autorizadas._**
Sets the Spanish standard road-transport envelope (2.55 m wide × 4 m high × 16.5 m articulated long, 40 t gross, with the 2024 update to 44 t for combined transport). "Genérica" oversize permits are required above 2.55 m wide / 4 m high / 16.5 m long. This bounds the maximum-module size for road transport into Catalan job sites.
Primary reference (third-party summary of RDs): https://fernandezaedo.com/en/spain-updates-road-transport-regulations-new-maximum-weights-and-dimensions-allowed/
Verified 2026-05-04. Bias: legal blog summarising the RD; primary statute available via BOE. Cross-checked with Nooteboom Trailers Spain rules page.

**[Sub-8] Catalonia Trade & Investment. (n.d.). _Strategic Location in Southern Europe._**
Catalonia's logistics envelope: 1 800 km regional rail, 400 km high-speed rail, 48-hour reach to 400 M consumers across Europe. Useful for establishing rail-truck combined-mode plausibility for CLT and modular components from central/northern Europe.
Primary URL: https://catalonia.com/why-catalonia/strategic-location-in-southern-europe
Verified 2026-05-04.

## Sub-strand 9 — Jobs / employment impact

**[Sub-9] Instituto Nacional de Estadística (INE). (2024 Q1). _Encuesta de Población Activa — sector construcción._**
Spanish National Statistics Institute labour-force data. Total Spanish unemployment 13.1 % in 2024 Q1; construction-sector unemployment 14.8 % (still 1.7 pp above the national average a decade-and-a-half after the post-2008 collapse).
Primary URL: https://www.ine.es/dyngs/INEbase/en/operacion.htm?c=Estadistica_C&cid=1254736176918
Verified 2026-05-04. Bias: official statistics, low; sectoral disaggregation methodology stable.

**[Sub-9] OECD. (2024). _OECD Employment Outlook 2024 — Country Notes: Spain._**
Independent benchmark on Spanish labour-market dynamics 2008-2024.
Primary URL: https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-employment-outlook-2024-country-notes_d6c84475-en/spain_b237abc0-en.html
Verified 2026-05-04.

**[Sub-9] McKinsey & Company. (2019). _Modular construction: From projects to products._**
Industry-standard quantitative reference: up to 80 % of activity moves off-site; factory labour ~2× as productive as in-situ; total labour cost reduction ~20–25 %. Caveat: McKinsey services modular-industry clients; its baseline is a North-American industry that is more labour-cost-sensitive than Spain.
Primary URL: https://www.mckinsey.com/~/media/mckinsey/business%20functions/operations/our%20insights/modular%20construction%20from%20projects%20to%20products%20new/modular-construction-from-projects-to-products-full-report-new.pdf
Verified 2026-05-04. **Bias direction: industry-favourable (LOWBALL on labour-displacement effects).**

**[Sub-9] World Economic Forum. (2025, January). _How modular construction drives productivity and circularity._**
Reports Europe-wide finding that modular projects use ~30 % fewer on-site labour hours, with ~80 % of construction activity factory-shifted. Sweden ~45 % of new housing offsite.
Primary URL: https://www.weforum.org/stories/2025/01/modular-construction-productivity-circularity/
Verified 2026-05-04. Bias: WEF, broadly aligned with industry framing — LOWBALL on displacement.

**[Sub-9] Hossain, M. U., Bekiri, F., Ng, S. T., & Pan, W. (2020). A Review of 3D Printing in Construction and its Impact on the Labor Market. _Sustainability, 12_(20), 8492. https://doi.org/10.3390/su12208492**
Peer-reviewed review summarising labour-cost reduction figures from the 3DPC literature: 50–80 % labour-cost reduction, 30–60 % waste reduction, 50–70 % time reduction. The authors note these are headline figures from studies that mostly count direct on-site bricklayers + general labour but rarely the offsetting tech-and-engineering FTE.
Verified 2026-05-04.
**Bias direction: study summarises early-2020 vendor-supplied figures, BIASED HIGH on displacement (counts only on-site labour reduction, undercounts factory + machine-tending uplift).**

**[Sub-9] International Labour Organization. (2018). _The Future of Jobs at Risk of Automation (Working Paper, ed_dialogue/act_emp)._**
ILO global review of automation-risk by sector. Construction is identified as one of the highest-automation-risk industries because it combines high routinisable manual content with low capital-intensity. The paper explicitly cautions that displacement is uneven: low-skill on-site labour (immigrant, often informally contracted) bears the brunt.
Primary URL: https://www.ilo.org/sites/default/files/wcmsp5/groups/public/@ed_dialogue/@act_emp/documents/publication/wcms_579554.pdf
Verified 2026-05-04. **Bias direction: ILO is the worker-protection multilateral; treat its narrative framing as labour-protective, but quantitative claims are derived from peer-reviewed Frey-and-Osborne-style methodology.**

**[Sub-9] ILO. (2025). _Generative AI and Jobs: A Refined Global Index of Occupational Exposure (Working Paper 140)._**
Updates the 2023 ILO global GenAI exposure index. Construction-related occupational categories show relatively low GenAI exposure but high physical-automation exposure, supporting the framing that 3DPC + modular are the displacing technologies, not GenAI.
Primary URL: https://www.ilo.org/sites/default/files/2025-05/WP140_web.pdf
Verified 2026-05-04. **Bias direction: same as 2018 paper.**

**[Sub-9] Banco Bilbao Vizcaya Argentaria (BBVA) Research. (n.d.). _Spain — Construction is facing a productivity problem._**
Reports labour-productivity drop of >20 % since 2013 in Spanish construction (GVA per hour worked, real terms). Frames the structural problem as a shortage of specialised training, _not_ a shortage of labour. Important for the politics of displacement: Spain's sector is under-trained, not over-staffed.
Primary URL: https://www.bbvaresearch.com/en/publicaciones/spain-construction-is-facing-a-productivity-problem/
Verified 2026-05-04. **Bias direction: bank-research, BIASED toward productivity-and-investment narrative; numerically reliable.**

**[Sub-9] CaixaBank Research. (n.d.). _Productivity in Spain: a lot of scope for improvement._**
Spanish construction-labour productivity reported at ~€112 400 per FTE per year (2023). Cross-check for FTE-per-100m² estimates.
Primary URL: https://www.caixabankresearch.com/en/economics-markets/activity-growth/productivity-spain-lot-scope-improvement
Verified 2026-05-04.

**[Sub-9] Apis Cor / Dezeen. (2019, December 22). _Apis Cor builds world's largest 3D-printed building in Dubai._**
Documents that the 640 m², 9.5 m two-storey Dubai Municipality administrative building was 3D-printed using "about half the usual number of construction workers, only 15." A primary anecdotal datum for the on-site-FTE-reduction range claimed for 3DPC.
Primary URL: https://www.dezeen.com/2019/12/22/apis-cor-worlds-largest-3d-printed-building-dubai/
Verified 2026-05-04. **Bias direction: vendor-favourable case (LOWBALL on full-system FTE).**

## Sub-strand 10 — Remaining 1.5 °C carbon budget

**[Sub-10] Friedlingstein, P., et al. & Global Carbon Project. (2025, November). _Global Carbon Budget 2025 — FAQs._**
Latest authoritative annual update (data through 2024 + 2025 projections). 2025 fossil-fuel emissions: 38.1 Gt CO₂; land-use 4.1 Gt; total +1.1 % vs 2024. Remaining budgets at start of 2025: 1.5 °C — _virtually exhausted_ (50 % chance breached within 4 years at current rate); 1.7 °C — 525 Gt CO₂ (~12 yr); 2.0 °C — 1 055 Gt CO₂ (~25 yr). Released 2025-11.
Primary URL: https://globalcarbonbudget.org/gcb-2025/the-global-carbon-budget-faqs-2025/
Verified 2026-05-04.

**[Sub-10] Friedlingstein, P., et al. (2025). _Global Carbon Budget 2024 — Earth System Science Data._** (Companion peer-reviewed publication.)
URL: https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/17/965/2025/
Verified 2026-05-04.

**[Sub-10] UNEP & Global Alliance for Buildings and Construction. (2025, March). _Global Status Report for Buildings and Construction 2024/25._**
The buildings-and-construction sector accounts for 32 % of global energy consumption and 34 % of global energy-and-process CO₂ emissions in 2023. Construction (embodied) emissions for cement, steel, aluminium ≈ 2.5 Gt CO₂/yr; brick + glass ≈ 1.2 Gt CO₂/yr. New construction adds ~7 % of global emissions (~2.5 Gt/yr). Sector grew 5 % since 2015; 28 % reduction by 2030 needed for Paris alignment. 2023 was the first year of decoupling: emissions flat while floor-area grew.
Primary URL: https://globalabc.org/sites/default/files/2025-03/Global-Status-Report-2024_2025.pdf (and UNEP main page https://www.unep.org/resources/report/global-status-report-buildings-and-construction-20242025)
Verified 2026-05-04.

**[Sub-10] International Energy Agency. (2024). _Buildings — IEA Energy System tracker._**
Confirms the 37 % buildings-share-of-energy-and-process-CO₂ figure used by UNEP. Provides operational vs embodied disaggregation: buildings ≈ 27 % operational, materials-and-construction ≈ 10 % embodied (Architecture 2030 recompiles this as 27 % operational + 15 % materials/construction, which aggregates infrastructure beyond buildings).
Primary URL: https://www.iea.org/energy-system/buildings
Verified 2026-05-04.

**[Sub-10] Architecture 2030. (2024). _The Current 1.5 °C Budget._**
Restates IPCC AR6 figures and the implied built-environment trajectory. Cites embodied carbon of structure + sub-structure + enclosures = 11 % of global GHG and 28 % of buildings-sector GHG annually.
Primary URL: https://www.architecture2030.org/the-current-1-5c-budget/
Verified 2026-05-04. Bias: advocacy organisation, but quantitative claims trace to IPCC AR6 / IEA primary sources.

**[Sub-10] IPCC. (2023). _AR6 Synthesis Report: Climate Change 2023._**
The most-recent IPCC synthesis. Summarises post-2020 budget figures: 500 Gt CO₂ for 50 % chance of 1.5 °C from start-2020; reduced by ~280 Gt CO₂ of subsequent emissions through 2024 → ≈ 220 Gt remaining at start-2025; further consumed in 2025 to leave the "virtually exhausted" headline figure in Global Carbon Budget 2025.
Primary URL: https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/syr/
Verified 2026-05-04.

## Rejected sources (transparency log)

- **Carbon Brief (analysis pieces).** Excellent journalism but secondary; replaced wherever possible with the Global Carbon Project / UNEP / IPCC primary documents.
- **3D Printing Industry / 3dprintingindustry.com headlines (e.g. "90% of jobs could be lost").** Click-bait framing; the original Frey & Osborne (2013) figure they cite is methodologically contested and not construction-specific. Excluded from sub-strand 9 as primary; noted in synthesis as evidence of the high-end-of-displacement narrative.
- **TradingEconomics / Statista country-statistics aggregators.** Useful for orientation but rejected for primary citation because the underlying data is INE / Eurostat — cited instead at source.
- **"89% Hispalyt market share" claim circulated in the project brief.** Could not be verified at primary URL on 2026-05-04. The Hispalyt-published figure verified here is 85 %. Recorded as a discrepancy.
- **Hispalyt EPD A4 baseline of 87 km.** The brief stated this as verified; could not be re-verified in this session because the GlobalEPD 008-016 PDF returned as binary and was not text-extractable. Listed as UNKNOWN with `source = NA` in VALUES.md sub-table 8 to satisfy the iron rule.
