## Anchor case 1 — Re:Crete (EPFL SXL)

**Devènes, J., Brütting, J., Küpfer, C., Bastien-Masse, M., & Fivet, C.** (2022). Re:Crete — Reuse of concrete blocks from cast-in-place building to arch footbridge. *Structures*, *43*, 1854–1867. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.istruc.2022.07.012

- **Tier 1.** Peer-reviewed journal article (Elsevier). Confirmed via EPFL Infoscience record `https://infoscience.epfl.ch/record/295996` and SXL project page `https://www.epfl.ch/labs/sxl/research/reuse-of-concrete/`.
- **Anchor finding.** The Re:Crete arch (10 m span, 25 saw-cut concrete blocks reassembled with mortar joints + post-tensioning, opened 11 October 2021, bluefactory Fribourg, CH) shows **−71 % GWP vs recycled-concrete-block arch, −63 % vs monolithic recycled-concrete arch, −74 % vs steel-beam arch, +9 % vs glulam-timber arch.**
- **Methodology disclosure.** Transport accounts for 34 % of the Re:Crete arch's GWP; timber centring for another 34 %; mortar 17 %; post-tensioning 9 %; sawing negligible. Transport emission factor used: ~1 kg CO₂eq per km for the 25-block load. SXL discloses sustainability advocacy bias in framing.
- **Note on year mismatch with brief.** The strand brief listed "(2021)" but the peer-reviewed paper is 2022. The 2021 date is the *prototype opening*, not publication. The 2022 paper is the citable source.
- **Why this is the anchor.** Re:Crete is the most rigorously published reuse-LCA precedent in the EU, with phase-resolved GWP breakdown and transport sensitivity reported.

## Anchor case 2 — K.118 / Halle 118 (baubüro in situ)

**baubüro in situ AG.** (2021). *K.118 / Kopfbau Halle 118, Winterthur* [Project documentation]. https://www.insitu.ch/projekte/196-k118-kopfbau-halle-118

- **Tier 2.** Institutional project documentation; the LCA itself is published in the De Wolf et al. (2020) peer-reviewed companion paper (see below).
- **Anchor finding.** Three-storey extension on the Sulzer site; **1,266 m² GFA**; ~60 % GHG reduction vs equivalent new construction; **500 t primary materials saved**; ~70 % component reuse rate. Steel skeleton sourced Basel, exterior staircase / windows / granite-flooring sourced Zürich (sourcing radius < 150 km within Swiss Mittelland).
- **Awards.** Prix Acier 2021; Holcim Award for Sustainable Construction (Global + European Gold).
- **Why this is the anchor.** K.118 is the only completed full-building reuse LCA at this scale in Europe; it is the case study that the De Wolf et al. (2020) allocation paper rebuilt from raw inventory data.

## Methodological foundation — allocation rules

**De Wolf, C., Hoxha, E., & Fivet, C.** (2020). Comparison of environmental assessment methods when reusing building components: A case study. *Sustainable Cities and Society*, *61*, 102322. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scs.2020.102322

- **Tier 1.** Peer-reviewed (Elsevier). Verified via Infoscience `https://infoscience.epfl.ch/entities/publication/6af4d52a-e4bd-44fb-9438-f13afb7048e7` and TU Graz mirror.
- **Load-bearing finding.** Six allocation methods compared on the K.118 inventory: cut-off, end-of-life, PAS-2050 distributed allocation, Environmental Footprint, degressive, and SIA 2032. Authors conclude "discrepancies between methods are so high that LCA is not sufficient to measure the environmental quality of reuse practices." This is the citation that licenses the installation's methodology-wobble overlay.
- **Why this is mandatory.** Without this paper, the installation cannot defend a single GWP number for reclaimed brick — the 50 % allocation-rule swing is the finding, not a footnote.

## Reclaimed-brick LCA primary literature

**Salmio, E., & Huuhka, S.** (2026). Environmental impacts of reclaimed bricks: comparing different deconstruction methods. *Buildings & Cities*, *7*(1), 323–340. https://doi.org/10.5334/bc.651

- **Tier 1.** Peer-reviewed open-access journal (Ubiquity Press). Verified at primary URL.
- **Key numbers.** Declared unit: 1 t whole reclaimed bricks on pallets. **GWP-total reduction vs new bricks: 95 % (T1, hand-held demolition hammer) / 94 % (T2, excavator + grapple). GWP-fossil reduction: 88 % (T1) / 86 % (T2).** Whole-brick yield: 38 % (T1) vs 24 % (T2). Labour: T1 = 1.4 hours for 2 workers per tonne; sorting/cleaning/packing ≈ 4 labour-hours per tonne. Transport baseline 30 km (Helsinki); reclaimed-bricks remain net-beneficial up to 480 km (T1) / 315 km (T2). Allocation rule: **cut-off**, with deconstruction burden allocated to the donor building (i.e. second life inherits zero upstream production burden).
- **Why this matters.** This is the most rigorous reclaimed-brick LCA in print. It also reverses the assumption that cement-bound modern bricks cannot be reclaimed — they can, with reduced yield.

**Devos, T., et al.** (2024). Reclaimed or new? Life cycle assessment of ceramic bricks. *Journal of Cleaner Production*. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclepro.2024.143XXX (locator approximate; verified at `https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S095965262403213X`)

- **Tier 1.** Peer-reviewed (Elsevier). Article number S095965262403213X.
- **Key numbers.** Reuse of 1 m² of brick wall yields **85–86 % environmental gain vs new bricks**. Deconstruction wastage **30–70 % of total mass** depending on brick + mortar combination. Cement-mortar bricks yield lower; lime-mortar bricks (typically pre-1940s buildings) yield higher.
- **Why this matters.** Independent confirmation of Salmio & Huuhka (2026) at one-m² spatial scale; named the cement-vs-lime mortar wobble explicitly.
- **Caveat.** Full-text not retrieved at primary source within the time budget — the 85–86 % figure is reproduced from the abstract / cross-citation in Salmio & Huuhka (2026); the exact DOI suffix should be confirmed before any final report cites it.

## Reclaimed-brick EPD (industry-grade)

**Reclaimed Brick Company UK.** (2024). *Environmental Product Declaration — Reclaimed Bricks (EN 15804+A2 / ISO 14025)*. Reclaimed Brick Company. https://reclaimedbrickcompany.co.uk/pages/environmental-product-declaration

- **Tier 1** (third-party verified EPD; first UK EPD for reclaimed bricks).
- **Key numbers.** **A1–A3 GWP = 20.4 kg CO₂eq per tonne** (vs ~250–350 kg CO₂eq per tonne for new fired bricks). Processing energy 138 kWh/t. Total lifecycle GWP (A–C) = **−3.18 kg CO₂eq per tonne** (negative; reflects avoided-burden methodology). Reference Service Life 150 yr. End-of-life assumption: 70 % can be re-reclaimed.
- **Allocation rule.** **Avoided burden** for the total lifecycle figure; **cut-off** is implicit for the A1–A3 number (zero upstream burden). This is the ONE source where the same product is reported under two allocation rules, demonstrating the wobble in real product data.
- **Caveat.** UK supply chain — not directly applicable to Catalonia, but it is the only verified EPD in print for reclaimed brick.

## Stock-matching foundation

**Brütting, J., Senatore, G., Schevenels, M., & Fivet, C.** (2020). Optimum design of frame structures from a stock of reclaimed elements. *Frontiers in Built Environment*, *6*, 57. https://doi.org/10.3389/fbuil.2020.00057

- **Tier 1.** Peer-reviewed open-access (Frontiers). Verified at primary URL.
- **Relevance.** Establishes the stock-matching problem formulation: minimising environmental impact of a structural frame subject to an inventory of reclaimed elements with fixed dimensions. Process impacts include deconstruction, reconditioning, and transport of reclaimed elements. This is the methodological backbone behind both Re:Crete and K.118 sourcing logic.

**Küpfer, C., Bastien-Masse, M., & Fivet, C.** (2023). Reuse of concrete components in new construction projects: Critical review of 77 circular precedents. *Journal of Cleaner Production*, *383*, 135235. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclepro.2022.135235

- **Tier 1.** Peer-reviewed (Elsevier). Verified at Infoscience.
- **Relevance.** Reviews 77 reuse-of-concrete projects (1967–2022) in EU + USA. Survivorship-bias caveat: these are documented showcase precedents, not the average. Useful for naming the bias explicitly.

## Catalan / Spanish baseline

**Institut de Tecnologia de la Construcció de Catalunya (ITeC).** (2026). *BEDEC — Banc Estructurat de Dades d'Elements Constructius* [Database, edition 2026]. https://en.itec.cat/database/

- **Tier 1** (Catalan official construction-data bank; aligned with EN 15804+A2).
- **Relevance.** BEDEC 2026 carries 22 environmental indicators per element following EN 15804+A2 — including GWP, recycled-content %, and waste output. Catalonia mandates ≥ 5 % recycled aggregate from C&D waste in public works (Catalan Govt., 2021). However, **BEDEC does not currently publish a "ladrillo recuperado" / "totxo recuperat" entry with verified GWP** — Catalan baseline reclaimed-brick data is a gap.
- **Why this matters.** Establishes the regulatory floor and confirms that a Catalan reclaimed-brick LCA dataset does not yet exist; we must rely on EU-wide proxies (Salmio & Huuhka, Devos et al., RBC EPD) and disclose the geographic-transfer caveat.

## Marketplace / cost references

**Concular GmbH.** (2026). *Urban Mining Hub Berlin — reclaimed-materials marketplace* [Online catalogue]. https://concular.de/urban-mining-hub/

- **Tier 2.** Institutional / company-published; specific brick-pricing data not exposed in public catalogue (sales@concular.de quote required).
- **Relevance.** Documents the German urban-mining infrastructure (5 hubs: Berlin, Aachen, Munich, Frankfurt, Merseburg + storage Düsseldorf, Stuttgart; Berlin facility 1,000 m²). The contrast against Catalonia — which has no equivalent network — anchors the "geographic-transfer" bias.

**Restado** (Concular subsidiary). (2026). *Marketplace for the reuse of construction material* [European Circular Economy Stakeholder Platform good-practice entry]. https://circulareconomy.europa.eu/platform/en/dialogue/existing-eu-platforms/restado

- **Tier 2.** EU-platform-listed.
- **Relevance.** Largest EU reclaimed-materials marketplace: ~1 M items, ~€40 M total stock value. Inventory includes bricks, wood, tiles, windows, doors. Operates primarily in DACH (Germany / Austria / Switzerland).

## Catalonian / Iberian context

**Institut d'Arquitectura Avançada de Catalunya (IAAC).** (2024). *Material mining in Barcelona* [Research project]. https://www.iaacblog.com/programs/material-mining-barcelona/

- **Tier 2.** Academic-institutional. Confirms Barcelona has a material-stock mapping research effort but no operational reclaimed-brick supply chain.
- **Companion source.** UIA 2026 World Capital of Architecture "MINERAL — Architects of Urban Mining" challenge (`https://uia2026bcn.org/winners-announced-for-the-mineral-urban-mining-architecture-challenge/`) confirms the gap is recognised at policy level. Catalonia generated ≥ 5 Mt C&D waste in 2019 (≈ 2× the 2013 figure).
